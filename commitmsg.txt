Stop the log stream drowning the 500 alert

Graylog raises Slack off container stdout, and a week of production
stdout is not something a human can read: 152,128 messages, of which a
third are one repeated 404 and most of the error-level remainder is a
single Redis incident saying the same thing hundreds of times. The
alert channel built on it stopped being read, which is the actual
outage here.

Two filters on the console handler, both narrow.

The self-healing throttle now applies to stdout, not only to Sentry.
sentry_config already knew that a dramatiq broker reconnect and a
swallowed django-redis cache error are one fault reported once a
second, and already collapsed them to one report per five minutes per
fault - but only in before_send. The log stream, which is the louder
path and the one on-call actually sees, kept every line. Same taxonomy,
same window length, its own window: a handler filter cannot see what
before_send decided, and sharing state would mean whichever consumer
asked first silently spent the other's report.

Keeping the first line of each fault is what makes this safe to put on
a handler. An outage that lasts is still reported every five minutes
for as long as it lasts, so the throttle reduces volume without
reducing coverage - and it gives Graylog a usable definition of "still
broken" (more than one of these in ten minutes) that a raw line count
could never provide.

Django's "Not Found: /api/v1/internal/domains" is dropped. That is
Caddy's on-demand TLS ask endpoint, where 404 is the documented way to
answer "do not issue a certificate for this SNI" - the endpoint working,
logged by django.request as though it had failed, once for every
scanner that opens a TLS connection to our IP. 49,835 lines in seven
days, one every twelve seconds, a third of the entire stream. Nothing
is lost: check_domain_allowed already logs each denial with the domain
that was refused, deduplicated by the resolve cache, and that line
stays. Scoped to django.request at WARNING, so a 5xx from the same
endpoint - which would mean no custom domain can get a certificate -
still surfaces.

Verified end to end against the real LOGGING config: nine log calls
covering both cases emit three lines, and the three kept are a
different 404, a 500 on the ask path, and the first line of the Redis
outage.

Refs SIRI-149.
