{% comment %} One certification the workspace holds, shown as a seal: ISO 27001, SOC 2 Type II, whatever they have published the artifact for. It is a tile rather than another c-branded.badge because the two say different things. A badge is a word beside a record, read in passing. This is the record: a visitor who came to the trust center to find out whether we are certified should land on the seal, read what it means, and click through to the report. So it gets a panel, a caption and a hit area, and it nests c-branded.surface rather than restating the panel recipe. The seal is an image the workspace's certifications supply, so it is passed in rather than chosen here, and it is decorative: the label underneath already says what it is, and a seal announced twice is noise in a screen reader. A tile with no image still renders, with the label doing the work, because a certification we have no artwork for is better shown than hidden. The whole tile is clickable through the heading's stretched pseudo element, which is the same trick c-branded.list-row uses and for the same reason: the legacy public stylesheet repaints any anchor it does not recognise, so it gets one element rather than every word in the tile. Not branded, and text-align comes through an arbitrary value: the brand is a fill under measured ink and a seal is read, not acted on, while the legacy sheets carry their own !important .text-center. {% endcomment %}
