{% comment %} One record in a list: an advisory, a document, a product. It goes inside c-branded.surface flush, which owns the radius and therefore the clipping. The row takes its content as named slots rather than as text props, and wraps each one itself. That is deliberate. The alternative is a general text component the page styles through props, and then every page picks its own combination and the list stops looking like one thing. Here the page supplies words and the component supplies the typography, which is what "pages compose, they do not style" means for a record. The severity spine is a real element rather than a left border, because a border would follow the row's box and the first and last rows sit against the panel's rounded corners. It is absolutely positioned across the row's full height and the panel clips it: a square bar against a round corner, with the clip the only thing stopping the colour spilling past. Its colour is the platform severity ramp, delivered as --tone so the utilities name it once, and it is never brandable. A linked row wraps only its heading in the anchor and stretches that anchor over the row with a pseudo element. The whole row is clickable, the link still has the heading as its accessible name, and, crucially, the legacy public stylesheet that repaints any anchor it does not recognise gets one element to repaint rather than every word in the row. The heading restates its own colour to win that fight. The hover tint is the brand at low alpha behind text that stays neutral, which is the one safe way a row may wear a workspace colour. {% endcomment %}
