{% comment %} The panel branded content sits on. It is deliberately not brandable: surface, border and radius come from the platform tokens, so however a workspace colours its accents, the ground the reader reads against stays legible and every trust centre keeps the same shape. That is the whole rule of this library in one component. Colour goes on the things a reader acts on, never on the ground they read against. flush drops the inset padding, for a panel whose rows own their own, and is written as the inverted flag rather than padded="True" because an omitted cotton prop is undefined, not False, so a positive default cannot be tested. A flush panel also clips. Its children run to the edge, and a child that paints there is square while this corner is not, so without the clip a severity spine or a row highlight draws over the radius and the colour spills past the corner. The panel owns the radius, so the panel owns the clipping: a caller cannot be relied on to remember. overflow-clip rather than overflow-hidden because .overflow-hidden is one of the legacy !important names, and clip is the right one anyway since nothing in a panel scrolls. Padded panels are not clipped, so a focus ring on a control inside one is still allowed to overhang. {% endcomment %}
